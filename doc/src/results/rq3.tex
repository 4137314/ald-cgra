\subsection{RQ3: residency, resources and verification effort}
\label{sec:rq3}

\emph{Communication.} The fused legacy dot product sends one 24-byte
exchange per element: 4 bytes of operands, 12 bytes of unused edge words
(the payload always carries $R+C$ words) and 8 bytes of command, step,
flag, mask, checksum and status. Uploading both vectors in 64-word frames
before a single computation removes the padding and most per-element
overhead. Fig.~\ref{fig:reuse} separates this batching effect from reuse
across calls. For eight dot products of length 256, the fused legacy kernel
transfers \ReuseStreamBytes{} bytes in \ReuseStreamJ{} transactions;
re-uploading both vectors for every call (no reuse) needs
\ReuseNoneBytes{} bytes in \ReuseNoneJ{} transactions, a
$\ReuseNoneFactor\times$ reduction attributable to batching and to sending
only the needed operands; reusing one vector needs \ReuseOneBytes{} bytes;
reusing both needs \ReuseBothBytes{} bytes in \ReuseBothJ{} transactions
($\ReuseBothFactor\times$ fewer bytes, $-\ReuseBothPercent\%$, and
$\ReuseBothJFactor\times$ fewer transactions). The last case repeats an
identical computation on identical data, whose result the host could have
cached; it measures the amortisation of uploads, not a realistic workload.
Resident byte counts depend on the data through byte stuffing (an archived
run with different operands counted 1\,856 instead of 1\,857 bytes). Since
the device term is negligible (RQ1), the time benefit predicted by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:time} follows the byte and transaction reductions, but no
speedup was measured.

\emph{Resources.} Enabling the extension adds $+68.6\%$ LUTs and $+320.5\%$
flip-flops after synthesis (Table~\ref{tab:block-cost}). The archived
reports have no per-module breakdown; by construction the added state
comprises a 144-byte receive buffer, the cached response, CRC and session
registers and the operand registers, but their individual shares were not
measured. The two logical $1024\times16$ banks map to two RAMB18
primitives counted by Vivado as one block-RAM tile. Only one PE computes
during \texttt{DOT}, so residency trades array utilisation for traffic.

\emph{Verification effort.} The extension added four property families with
163 assertion cells (sequencer 24, memories 31, receiver 22, integrated
controller 86) and 15 positive proof tasks taking 175\,s of solver time in
the full suite, against 23 tasks and 237\,s for the entire legacy design
(Table~\ref{tab:formal}). Its integration also required two timing-driven
redesigns before routed timing closed (Section~\ref{sec:chain}). These
figures are a coarse proxy of effort, not a complexity metric.
