\subsection{Failure contracts of the legacy protocol}
\label{sec:contracts}

The legacy protocol has no delimiter or sequence number, and its 8-bit
additive checksum detects only some corruptions. Its observable contract,
checked by differential replay against the RTL, is deliberately weak.
\emph{(i)}~\cmd{CFG} and \cmd{WR} commit words as they arrive, before the
checksum is validated: a \cmd{NACK} reports a failed transfer but does not
roll back. A rejected \cmd{EXEC} likewise latches its inputs, but neither
clears nor steps the array. \emph{(ii)}~Clearing the datapath (\cmd{RST} or
the \cmd{EXEC} flag) and restoring the parser to its idle state are different
operations: only a device reset or the receive watchdog ($10^{8}$ clocks,
1\,s at 100\,MHz) returns a parser that waits for payload bytes to idle.
\emph{(iii)}~A checksum error inside an intact frame is recoverable; loss of
framing (an inserted, dropped or corrupted length-defining byte) is not
detected in general. The library therefore retransmits only after a
complete, checksum-valid \cmd{NACK} of \cmd{CFG}, \cmd{WR} or a resetting
\cmd{EXEC}; a timeout, I/O error or malformed reply poisons the handle, and
later calls fail without sending bytes. Reopening the serial port does not
reset the device.
