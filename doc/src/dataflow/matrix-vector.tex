\subsection{Stationary coefficients with host reduction}

For $y=Ax$, the engine tiles $A$ into $R\times C$ blocks. For each column tile,
row~0 holds the corresponding coefficients of $x$ in its configuration
immediates. Each step injects one matrix row from the north: row~0 multiplies,
and rows~1 through $R-1$ pass the preceding products southward.
After $R$ steps, the snapshot satisfies
\begin{equation}
  q_{R-1-i,c}=A_{i,c}x_c,\qquad
  y_i\mathrel{+}=\sum_{c=0}^{C-1}q_{R-1-i,c},
\end{equation}
with local tile indices and zero padding outside the matrix. The host performs
the sum and accumulates across column tiles; it is part of the algorithm.

On a full tile, $C$ PEs perform useful multiplications per step and
$(R-1)C$ serve as product storage/forwarding. Thus a $4\times4$ mesh has four
active multipliers and twelve delay cells in this mapping, not sixteen useful
MACs per step. The steady full-tile multiplier utilisation is $1/R$;
padding and skipped tiles change the effective utilisation for a workload.
The term weight-stationary refers here specifically to $x$ held in row~0's
immediates; the complete computation includes host reduction.
