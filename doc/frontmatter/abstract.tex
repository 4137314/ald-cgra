\begin{abstract}
Small coarse-grained reconfigurable arrays (CGRAs) attached to a host over a
serial link are common in FPGA prototyping, yet their cost is usually
reported as fabric resources and clock frequency. We study a 16-bit,
step-controlled $R\times C$ CGRA ($RC\le16$) driven over UART, and ask three
questions: when fusing a write--run--read sequence into one transaction
pays for its extra bytes; how geometry and mapping determine traffic and
useful work; and how keeping operands resident on chip changes the balance
between communication, resources and verification effort. Exact byte,
transaction and step counts from the public API, checked against scalar
references and closed forms, feed a first-order link model. Fusion reduces
transactions by $1.99$--$3.95\times$ and is never slower for two of five
kernels; for the other three it wins only when the per-transaction
turnaround exceeds 58--174\,$\mu$s. Every mapping performs useful work in at
most one of $R$ PE-step slots, often one of 16, and on the default
$4\times4$ mesh the modelled device time is below $3\cdot10^{-5}$ of the
link time. Batched resident operands
reduce dot-product traffic $4.6\times$ without reuse and $26.9\times$ for
eight identical calls, at $+68.6\%$ LUTs and $+320\%$ flip-flops. Safety
properties are proved on the production VHDL, and the proved capture guards
are linked to the multicycle exceptions checked by static timing analysis.
All results are pre-silicon; no board measurement was performed.
\end{abstract}
